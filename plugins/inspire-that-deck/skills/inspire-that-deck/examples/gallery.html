Deck element gallery

Twelve small pieces from two cute deck styles, each shown working on its own. They are ideas to borrow, not a deck to fill in. Every piece says the moment it fits, when to leave it out, and what it must keep.

Point your AI here, then let it build from your content: one slide's real point first, then the one piece that helps say it.

All the example words are made up (a weekend bake sale).
  • Start from the point, not the piece.Pick an element because the slide has a moment it fits, like a decision, a quote or progress.
  • One star per slide.Tape, a stamp and a sprite on one slide fight each other. Choose one; let the rest stay plain.
  • Hard news stays plain.A worrying number, a loss or an apology gets clean type and no bounce.
  • Motion is a bonus, never the message.Each piece reads fully when the reader has asked for less motion.

Washi tape holding a card

Fits
One headline fact you want to feel pinned down and real.
Skip it when
The slide is a dense table: tape on every cell turns into noise.
Keep
The tape over a corner, never over words. Torn ends come from a clip-path, so no image is needed.

Polaroid with a pin

Fits
Moments: "what happened", one finding per photo, three or four in a row.
Skip it when
There is no picture to draw. An empty polaroid looks broken.
Keep
A short caption (one line, two at most) in the hand font, at a size people can read.

Sticky note that slaps on

Fits
A to-do or a "next time" that should feel like a reminder you would actually stick up.
Skip it when
You have more than one per slide: a wall of notes reads as clutter.
Keep
Six words or so. The slap is quick (under half a second) and happens once.

Torn paper for a quote

Fits
Someone's own words. The torn edge says "cut from the real thing".
Skip it when
The words are yours, not theirs. Then it is a claim, so set it plainly.
Keep
Who said it, under the quote. Quote exactly or not at all.

Stickers with a white edge

Fits
A small drawn icon next to a point, so the eye has somewhere to land. Hover one.
Skip it when
It would stand in for the point itself. An icon is a signpost, not the message.
Keep
Drawn SVG, never an emoji (emoji look different on every computer). The white edge is four stacked drop-shadows.

A stamp lands on a decision

Fits
The one thing everyone agreed. It marks the moment talk became a decision.
Skip it when
Nothing was decided yet. A stamp on an open question claims too much.
Keep
The stamp after the content has arrived, so people read first. One stamp per deck feels earned.

A checklist you tick live

Fits
Agreeing next steps in the room: tick each as people say yes. Click the boxes.
Skip it when
The deck is read alone, without you there to tick.
Keep
Real buttons, so a keyboard can tick them too. The tick springs in; the words stay readable when done.

Cozy quest

A little pixel game: a mascot, a dialogue box, progress you can earn. Playful; good for training, onboarding or a long deck that needs a reason to keep going.

A mascot that bobs

Fits
A guide who appears on the opening and closing slides and reacts to what happens.
Skip it when
The audience is meeting you for a serious first time and does not know the joke yet.
Keep
Two frames, stepped, not smooth: that is what makes it read as pixel. Sharp edges via crisp rendering.

Dialogue box that types itself

Fits
The mascot saying the slide's one point, the way a game character would.
Skip it when
The text is long. Typing out four lines makes people wait to read.
Keep
The full sentence in the page from the start (screen readers get it at once). The blinking arrow says "there is more".

An XP bar you fill by clicking

Fits
Progress through a list: one click per takeaway covered. It shows how far along everyone is.
Skip it when
The number is a real measure (sales, a score). A game bar makes a real figure look like a toy.
Keep
A text count next to the bar, and a progress role so the count is announced.

Collected so far

Inventory slots

Fits
A recap: each lesson so far is an item in the bag. Empty slots show what is still to come.
Skip it when
The items do not map to anything. A bag of random loot is decoration.
Keep
Items pop in one after another, a beat apart, so the eye can count them.
